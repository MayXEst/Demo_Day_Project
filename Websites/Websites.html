Coverage Websites

This is the starting point: figuring out which program you legally qualify for and signing up for it. These sites determine eligibility based on your income, age, household size, and state, then walk you through enrollment. Solving the coverage question first usually makes everything below cheaper or unnecessary.

Free and Sliding-Scale Clinics

This is where you go when you need to actually see a doctor and either have no insurance or can't wait for coverage to start. These are physical places that treat patients regardless of ability to pay — either free, or on a sliding fee scale tied to your income. They handle primary care, and many also cover dental, vision, and behavioral health.

Visit NACHC Website
Visit HRSA Website
Visit Free Clinics Website

For Prescriptions

Medication is billed separately from the visit, so having coverage or a free clinic doesn't automatically make drugs affordable. These fall into two types: assistance programs, where a manufacturer or nonprofit gives you the drug free or nearly free if you meet income criteria, and discount tools, which simply find you a lower cash price than the pharmacy's list price.

Visit NeedyMeds Website
Visit GoodRx Website
Visit RxAssist Website

Bills and financial help

This section is for after the care has happened and a bill exists — or when insurance covers the service but leaves you with copays, deductibles, and premiums you can't pay. These groups issue grants, negotiate on your behalf, and help you appeal denials.

Visit Patient Advocate Website
Visit NeedyMeds Website
Visit GoodRx Website

Advocacy and policy. Organizations promoting universal access

These don't provide care or money to individuals. They work on the system itself — lobbying, litigation, public campaigns, and research on why coverage gaps exist and what would close them. Useful if you want to understand the policy landscape, follow what's changing, or get involved rather than just get treated.
